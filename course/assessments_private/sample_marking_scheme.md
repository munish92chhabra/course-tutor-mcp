# MARKING SCHEME - Introductory Microbiology, Mid-Term

Internal. Not for release to students.

Q1. Peptidoglycan structure and the role of the wall. [6 marks]

Award up to 3 for structure: alternating NAG and NAM sugars forming glycan
chains (1); short peptide cross-bridges linking the chains (1); the result
being one continuous covalent mesh around the whole cell (1).
Award up to 3 for function: internal osmotic pressure of several atmospheres
in dilute medium (1); the wall resists it and prevents lysis (1); credit the
protoplast observation as evidence (1).
Full marks require both halves. A structure-only answer caps at 3.

Q2. Gram stain giving pink cells on a 48-hour Gram-positive culture. [5 marks]

Two explanations expected, 2 marks each, plus 1 for method.
Over-decolourisation: alcohol left on too long, thick wall dehydrates
incompletely and the crystal violet-iodine complex washes out.
Culture age: dying cells lose wall integrity, so the complex is not retained.
Confirmation (1 mark): repeat on a young exponential-phase culture with a
timed decolourisation step and a known control on the same slide.

Q3. Generation time calculation. [5 marks]

n = (log10 N - log10 N0) / 0.301 = (8 - 3) / 0.301 = 16.6 generations (3).
g = 360 min / 16.6 = 21.7 minutes, accept 21-22 (2).
Award method marks in full even where arithmetic slips, provided the working
is shown. No working, no method marks, even with the right number.

Q4. Lag phase as metabolically active. [4 marks]

One mark each, to a maximum of four: cell number constant but cells not
dormant; enzymes being induced for the new medium; damage repair; ribosome
and metabolite pools being built up. Credit the inoculum-age dependency as
an alternative fourth point.
A common weak answer says only "the cells are adjusting" - award 1.

Q5. Total versus viable count. [5 marks]

Definitions, 1 each. Viable count measures cells able to reproduce; total
count measures all cells present, living or dead.
Wrong-use situations, 1.5 each. Viable count is wrong where clumping matters,
since CFU counts an aggregate as one unit. Total count is wrong where only
living cells matter, for example assessing a disinfectant.

Q6. Limitations of OD600. [4 marks]

Two marks each for any two of: measures scattering rather than cells, so needs
calibration per organism and instrument; linear only below roughly 0.8; dead
cells still scatter, so OD does not fall in death phase; cell size and shape
changes alter scattering independently of number.

Q7. Superoxide dismutase and catalase. [5 marks]

Oxygen toxicity arises from partially reduced products, not O2 itself (1).
Superoxide dismutase converts superoxide to hydrogen peroxide (2).
Catalase converts hydrogen peroxide to water and oxygen (1).
Obligate anaerobes typically lack these enzymes, which is why oxygen is
lethal to them (1).
